Triple T: the (hopefully) native ternary asic

Triple T (t3 for short) is an experimental native ternary asic that uses balanced ternary logic (-1,0,+1) to perform arithmetic operations in place of standard binary math (0,1). The extra logic state allows for implicit signing and eliminates the need for dedicated subtraction circuitry inside the alu. T3 features an addition unit and multiplication unit, along with multiple tritwise(ternary bitwise) operands. Originally, t3 was intended to feature 3 threads, which was totally cooler than intel’s useless dual thread setup, but due to space constraints, this was soon dropped. Due to the nature of ternary, many unique operations are made accessible to the user, such as the ability to handle simultaneous adding of 4 inputs without generating additional digit of carry, and numerous use cases for ternary include the definition of ‘0’ as an uncertain state, a possible state, or an uninitialized state.
