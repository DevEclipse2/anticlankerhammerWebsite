the story of the development of T3 is one which is filled with turmoil from it's eureka moment of conception to it's turbulent development to the numberous comprimises made to Triple T

The pure idealism


Triple T was supposed to do it all, but the lack of experience in the area by the sole developer may have doomed it

The vision


Before the final version of triple T came to light, there were a multitude of changes made. for every glorious feature etched in silicon was one battered concept, shattered by reality. Initially, triple T was spec'd out to be one of the most capable in it's class, excelling in computation density, and leading in reliability. the turmoil in it's development cycle has left many versions of T3 scattered throughout sketches, poorly fashioned concepts, and other files Below are some of the most notable ones.

AZ-00-1 Nickname: SNUB


named after the initials of the researchers who developed the soviet Setun computer, this is the first appearance of ternary logic presented as a serious contender and a valid choice for the asic. All iterations below descend from it's lineage, and SSNB can be seen as the keystone of the entire project despite no further progress beyond a brainstorming stage, SNUB has set in motion events that will change the die space forever.

AZ-04-1 Nickname: Necro


necro was a significant departure in terms of design philosophy, aiming to maximize the chances of a functional / partially functional asic. by strategically placing cuts and reroutes near error prone components, Necro was able to cut off large sections of it's die from power routing. this in effect gave the ability for the core to ampututate itself, either to conserve power, or as a last ditch attempt to rid itself of a faulty macro. Multiple e-fuses surrounded the perimeter, able to be triggered from a central command and control unit. although Necron was redundant due to the implementation of more reliable subsystems, a shadow of it remains in the core commander we know and love today.
